# Fluid-Related Corrosion Investigation

**Real-World Quality Problem-Solving Case**  
*Industrial Manufacturing | Incoming Quality / Supplier Quality Environment*

> **Confidentiality:** This case study is anonymized. Company names, supplier names, internal identifiers, NCR numbers, specifications and other confidential information have been omitted.

## 1. Background

In September 2026, during incoming and pre-production quality activities, I observed a recurring corrosion issue affecting small bearing components and the rings used for connection with a shaft.

The corrosion appeared shortly after cleaning activities and had a distinctive streak-like pattern resembling liquid run-off.

No rain or external water exposure was present, and the corrosion appeared within approximately 30 minutes.

## 2. Initial Observation

I noticed visible corrosion on areas that had been cleaned before a lunch break.

The pattern looked like liquid streaks with corrosion.

Because there was no obvious environmental source of water, I considered the process fluid being used during cleaning as a possible cause.

Rather than assuming the cause, I decided to investigate the possibility.

## 3. Investigation

I:

- checked the identification, supplier and product code of the fluid being used,
- compared it with other available fluids,
- compared areas exposed to the suspected fluid with areas that were not,
- used a different fluid as a comparison,
- and observed whether the suspected effect could be reproduced.

The basic approach was:

**Observe → Form a hypothesis → Isolate the variable → Test → Compare → Escalate**

## 4. Practical Verification

I applied the suspected fluid to a comparable metallic area that had not previously been exposed to it.

A different fluid was used for comparison.

The suspected fluid produced visible corrosion, while the comparison fluid did not show the same effect.

The corrosion was reproduced again after reapplying the suspected fluid.

This provided practical evidence that the specific fluid was contributing to the corrosion.

## 5. Evidence and Escalation

I documented the observations and provided photographs, comparisons and my findings to the responsible:

- Quality Supplier Lead
- Quality Engineer
- Incoming Quality team

I explained how the comparison and practical test had been performed.

The responsible Quality team then verified the observation together with me.

The corrosion appeared again after approximately 10–20 minutes, but only in connection with the specific fluid.

## 6. Corrective Action

The responsible Quality organization confirmed that the fluid was contributing to the corrosion issue.

The existing stock of the affected fluid was replaced and its source/supplier was changed.

I did not have authority to change the supplier, modify the quality standard or formally approve the corrective action.

My responsibility was to identify the suspected cause, investigate it and provide evidence to the responsible Quality team.

## 7. Outcome

After the affected fluid was removed from use, I no longer observed the same corrosion issue associated with that fluid.

Other corrosion-related issues occurred later under different conditions, including material/casting-related factors, but those were outside the scope of this case.

## 8. My Contribution

My direct contribution was:

- identifying an unusual quality problem,
- recognizing a possible process-related cause,
- checking the relevant process information,
- comparing affected and unaffected conditions,
- isolating the suspected variable,
- reproducing the effect,
- documenting evidence,
- and escalating the findings to the responsible Quality team.

I did **not** own the formal supplier change or final corrective-action approval.

## 9. Quality Engineering Learning

This case strengthened my understanding of several Quality Engineering principles:

- **Observation is not the same as root cause**
- **A hypothesis should be tested**
- **Variables should be isolated where possible**
- **Evidence is stronger than assumption**
- **Verification is necessary before drawing conclusions**

The investigation also strengthened my interest in moving from hands-on quality inspection toward **structured problem solving and Quality Engineering**.
